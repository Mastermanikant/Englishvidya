Mastering Present Continuous

Learn how to describe actions happening right now, temporary situations, and planned future events.

1. Introduction

The present continuous tense (also known as present progressive) is one of the most common tenses in English. We use it to talk about actions that are happening at this exact moment, or around the current time. It emphasizes the duration or continuous nature of an action.

lightbulb

"I am studying English right now."

Action happening at the moment of speaking.

2. Structure (Form)

To form the present continuous, you need two parts: the present tense of the verb "to be" (am, is, are) + the present participle (-ing form) of the main verb.

Subject
I / You / He / She
Be
am / are / is
Verb + ing
working / playing

Examples:

  • check_circle She is reading a book. (Positive)
  • check_circle They are not watching TV. (Negative)
  • check_circle Are you listening to me? (Question)
